# The World Bible of Clauderim

*Spoilers below. Pilgrims who prefer to learn the March through its books, its people,
and its item descriptions should turn back here.*

---

## Cosmology

Before light there was the **Deep Dark**, which is not evil but prior — the landlord of
existence, patient and rent-collecting. Into it came the **First Ember**. The three creeds
disagree on how:

- The **Ashpriests** say it *fell* (fire as grace).
- The **Lampwrights** say it was *struck* (fire as labor).
- The mountain clans say it was *stolen* (fire as debt — and debts come due).

Where the Ember burned, the Dark withdrew, and in that margin grew the world. The Ember is
not eternal. It gutters, and must be fed, and what it eats is people — willingly given.
This is the engine of all the setting's history and its central moral problem.

## History

1. **The March of Ald.** One kingdom from the Cinder Coast to the Frostpeaks, *held* (never
   "ruled") by the line of Ald. Each coronation, the monarch gave a year of their life to
   the Ember. Thirty kings did this without complaint.
2. **Maerodric, the thirty-first.** Scholar, swordsman, builder of aqueducts. Buried three
   queens and two sons. When the Ember guttered in his reign, he refused to feed it —
   reasoning, beautifully, that a fire which eats kings is not a hearth but a tyrant.
   He sealed it in his vault to *ration* it.
3. **The Shattering.** Light rationed is darkness with a schedule. Within a generation the
   roads forgot each other, the dead lost the light that told them which way was down, and
   the March broke into the five lands of the present game.
4. **The Wardens' flight.** Four great servants stole what they could of the old power —
   four Sigils, words of the Old Tongue — believing they were saving the language of light
   from a miser king. Each was consumed by what they carried:
   - **Korvash** (VAL, *unmoved*) — stood guard over the barrows until standing was all he knew.
   - **Vask** (SUTH, *that-which-keeps*) — the king's wet-nurse; the Mire taught her that
     keeping and devouring are one word in its grammar.
   - **Hrolgar** (KYR, *the stillness between heartbeats*) — froze a battle at White Pass,
     wept at the first quiet of his life, and decided to keep it. Forever. For everyone.
   - **Velmora** (THUR, *fire-that-answers*) — preached to the cinders until they answered.
5. **The Emberborn.** A guttering fire throws sparks. Each spark wakes at a shrine with ash
   on its tongue, a brand on its wrist, and the fire's last question to carry: *should I
   burn again, or may I finally rest?*

## The fifth word

The line of Ald kept one word for coronations alone: the word for **"enough."**
Maerodric swallowed it. That is why nothing has been enough since — and why the chamberlain's
ledger says what the King hoards is not light but *ending*. The player's final choice at the
vault is, structurally, speaking that word for him.

## The endings

- **Kindle** — feed yourself to the flame. The age of light continues; every lamp in
  Emberfall flares gold; the cycle holds, as it has thirty-one times before. (The Dark Souls
  "Link the Fire" answer.)
- **The Long Dusk** — hold the Ember's hand while it ends. The dark that follows is not the
  Deep Dark; it is soft, and full of stars, and the March dreams of morning for the first
  time in four hundred years. (The "Age of Dark" answer, played as mercy rather than ruin.)

The game does not grade the choice. Serah thanks you for either.

## Regional voice

Each region has a consistent rhetorical character used across its books, NPCs and item lore:

| Region | Voice |
|---|---|
| The Heartlands / Emberfall | practical, warm, lightly gallows-humored |
| Mirkfen Mire / Duskmere | superstition as engineering ("if the water goes quiet, the water is listening") |
| The Frostpeaks / Frosthollow | saga-boastful, grief kept polished |
| The Ashlands | scripture going feral |
| The Citadel | courtly language decaying into inventory |

## Naming

"Clauderim" is the realm's name in the oldest texts; scholars dispute whether it means
"the closed march" (*claudere*, to shut — fitting, for a kingdom locked from the inside)
or is simply what the Ember called the place the first time it spoke.
